# Field guide: A morning briefing in Slack so your whole team starts the day on the same page

This guide explains everything shown in the "Ops Today" video: what each field and label means, what each teammate sees, and answers to common questions. **Everything on screen is sample data.** The guests, teammates, amounts and events are invented for the demo.

**Headline:** "A morning briefing in Slack so your whole team starts the day on the same page"

**Steps shown on screen:**
1. Every morning, the day's briefing is built automatically.
2. It posts to your team's Slack channel before the workday starts.
3. Alerts, bookings, reviews, money, guest messages, and cleaning coverage, all in one place.
4. Everyone sees the same plan, so loose ends are easier to catch.

## 1. Field guide

### The team chat window

| On screen | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Team chat** (window title) | A Slack-style team chat. It is drawn for the demo: no logo, no real workspace. |
| **La Maison** (sidebar) | The team's workspace name. |
| **Channels: # ops-today, # maintenance, # cleaning** | The team's channels. **# ops-today** is highlighted because that is where the briefing is posted. |
| **# ops-today** (channel header) | The channel everyone on the team can read. |
| **Sample data** tag | A reminder that every name, amount and event is invented. |
| **Ops Today · Tue 6/17 · yesterday's briefing · 8 sections** | Yesterday's post, faded, to show that a briefing arrives each morning. |

### The briefing post

| On screen | What it means |
|---|---|
| **La Maison APP** | The sender. The briefing is posted by an automated app account, not typed by a person. |
| **building…** / **Building · 6:58 AM** | While the post is still dashed, the briefing is being put together section by section. |
| **7:00 AM** | The time it was posted. Once posted, the dashed outline turns solid. |
| **Posted to #ops-today · 7:00 AM** | A short confirmation that appears when the post goes out. |
| **Ops Today · Wed 6/18** | The title: today's briefing and the date. |
| **Red alert** | The most urgent item of the day, shown first. Sample: "Hot tub heater fault reported at 6:40 AM. Omar D. is checking at 10 AM." It is an equipment issue, not a security alert. |
| **1 reply** | Someone replied in a thread under this item (here, the handyman). |
| **Bookings** | Today's check-outs and check-ins, plus new bookings. Sample: "Check-out: Dana K., 11 AM · Check-in: Sam R., 4 PM · New: Lee T., Jun 24–27". |
| **Reviews** | New guest reviews. Sample: "New 5-star review from Dana K.: 'Lovely garden, spotless kitchen.'" |
| **Money** | A rough money picture. Sample: "Payouts expected this week: about $2,400 · 1 contractor invoice due: $150". Amounts are round, made-up figures. |
| **Guest messages** | Guest messages still waiting on a reply. Sample: "2 waiting on a reply: Sam R. (parking), Lee T. (early check-in)". |
| **Worth a skim** | Lower-priority notes that are useful to know. Sample: "Street sweeping moved to Thu · Pool service moved to Fri". |
| **Cleaning coverage** | Who is cleaning today and which days still need someone. Sample: "Today 11 AM–3 PM: Maria G. · Sat 6/21: still needs a cleaner". |
| **Loose ends** | Small open tasks to follow up on. Sample: "Restock coffee pods · Recheck patio bulb (from inspection)". |
| **Seen by MG NL OD** | Teammates who have opened the post (initials for Maria G., Nina L., Omar D.). |
| **✓ 3** | A checkmark reaction, counting up as each teammate acknowledges the post. |

Each section has a small colored icon and a colored label so people can find their part at a glance. During step 3 the sections light up one by one to show that everything is in one post.

## 2. What each teammate sees

Everyone sees **the same post** in #ops-today. What differs is which part they are likely to act on:

- **Maria G. (cleaner)** gets the #ops-today notification. Her part is **Cleaning coverage**: "Turnover today, 11 AM–3 PM". She opens it (Seen ✓ 7:04 AM) and adds a checkmark.
- **Nina L. (inspector)** gets the same notification. Her part is **Loose ends**: "Recheck the patio bulb". She opens it (Seen ✓ 7:06 AM) and adds a checkmark.
- **Omar D. (handyman)** gets the same notification. His part is the **Red alert**: "Hot tub heater, 10 AM". He opens it (Seen ✓ 7:09 AM), adds a checkmark, and replies "On it, 10 AM", which shows up as "1 reply" on the red alert.
- **The owner or manager** sees the full post, plus who has seen it and who has replied.

## 3. FAQ

**When does the briefing arrive?**
In the demo it posts at 7:00 AM. The actual time depends on how the team sets it up, and it is usually timed for before the workday starts.

**Do I need a special app?**
Usually just the team's chat app (Slack in the real setup). The briefing is an ordinary channel post.

**Who can see the post?**
Generally, everyone in the #ops-today channel. Ask the owner or manager who is in the channel if you're unsure.

**Is there private information in it?**
The real briefing may include guest first names, booking dates and money figures, so the channel is usually limited to the team. The video uses invented sample data only.

**What if something in my part is wrong or out of date?**
Reply in a thread under that section or message the owner or manager. The briefing is a summary built from the team's records, so the source may need correcting.

**I can't make the job listed for me. What should I do?**
Reply on the post or contact the owner or manager as early as you can, so someone else can be found.

**Do I have to react or reply?**
That's up to the team. In the demo, a checkmark means "seen", and a reply is used when someone is taking an item.

**What is "Red alert"?**
The day's most urgent item, such as a broken appliance. It is listed first so people are more likely to notice it.

**What's the difference between "Worth a skim" and "Loose ends"?**
"Worth a skim" is useful background (schedule changes, reminders). "Loose ends" are small open tasks that may still need someone.

**Does it replace talking to each other?**
No. It gives everyone the same starting point, and questions can still go in the thread or directly to the owner or manager.

**Are the names and amounts real?**
No. Every name, guest, amount and event in the video is invented sample data.
